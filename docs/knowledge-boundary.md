# Knowledge and discovery boundary

The agent should learn and improve within a run. Shared knowledge may explain ordinary interface controls and general mechanics needed to interpret or use the game, such as supported gravship substructure, connected components and research requirements. This reference is sourced, scoped and retrieved when relevant.

Encounter-specific surprises, enemy behavior learned in battle, asset losses, tactical solutions and their consequences belong in the originating campaign. Do not preload those lessons into fresh runs, even after removing pawn names. The Continuance engine-loss lesson remains available for the rest of Continuance; it is not a default global warning. Generalization is a deliberate editorial decision subject to the user's discovery preference, never automatic promotion based on success or repeated use.

Preserve observed events, interpretation, uncertainty, decision, outcome and next retrieval trigger separately. Learning should affect later decisions in the same run and remain revisable. Missing context is a retrieval need, not evidence a problem disappeared. Global mechanics must not expose hidden current-game information or permit debug/balance/save changes.

Speed is part of quality: measure time back in play and useful information per model handover. Prefer one sufficient bounded response over smaller representations that predictably force decoding or another read. Retain novel fields, urgent changes, uncertainty and explicit recovery paths. Discovery pauses are justified when the current decision genuinely needs an unfamiliar mechanic or capability; avoid unbounded tooling work during play.
